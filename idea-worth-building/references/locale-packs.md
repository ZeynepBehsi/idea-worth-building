# Locale Packs — where to find local-market data

The analysis compares the user's **local market** with **global**. Use the generic procedure for any
country; the packs below are shortcuts. Verify source names and laws are current — organisations and
regulations change.

## Generic procedure (any country)

For the local market, find and use:
1. **Official statistics office** — population, businesses by sector, education, income. Search `{country} national statistics office {topic}`.
2. **Local app stores / marketplaces** — search the store in the local language; note local competitors and their reviews.
3. **Local complaint/review sites and forums** — search `{competitor} complaints {country}` and in the local language.
4. **Local startup/tech media and funding databases** — `{category} startup {country} funding`.
5. **Purchasing power** — GDP per capita (PPP) from the World Bank or IMF; typical local software/subscription prices.
6. **Currency** — pricing currency, inflation/volatility, exchange-rate date.
7. **Payments** — which local and global payment providers work for the founder's legal entity and the customer's cards.
8. **Data protection & sector rules** — the local data protection law plus sector rules (health, finance, education, minors).
9. **Language** — whether a local-language product is itself a moat (weak global competitors in that language).

## Türkiye (TR)
- Statistics: TÜİK; exams: ÖSYM; higher education: YÖK; business: TOBB; banking/fintech: BDDK; trade ministry reports.
- Complaints/reviews: Şikayetvar, Ekşi Sözlük, Google Play TR / App Store TR reviews.
- Startup media/data: Webrazzi, Egirişim, startups.watch.
- Payments: iyzico, PayTR, local bank POS; check Stripe availability for the founder's entity.
- Law: KVKK (data protection); sector rules for health, finance, education.
- Notes: high inflation and TRY volatility → consider USD/EUR pricing for global, TRY for local; B2C subscription willingness to pay tends to be lower; Turkish-language gaps in global products can be a moat.

## United States (US)
- Statistics: Census Bureau, BLS. Reviews: App Store/Google Play, G2, Capterra, Reddit, Trustpilot.
- Law: sectoral (HIPAA for health, COPPA for children), state privacy laws such as CCPA/CPRA.

## European Union (EU)
- Statistics: Eurostat + national offices. Law: GDPR, plus the EU AI Act for AI products, DSA for platforms.
- Notes: many languages → local-language products may face weaker competition per country but smaller markets.

## United Kingdom (UK)
- Statistics: ONS. Law: UK GDPR, Data Protection Act.

## India (IN)
- Statistics: MOSPI. Law: Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Notes: price-sensitive B2C, UPI payments dominant.

## Southeast Asia (e.g., Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines, Malaysia, Singapore)
- Statistics: each country's national statistics office (e.g., BPS in Indonesia, DOSM in Malaysia, SingStat in Singapore).
- Notes: super-apps and messaging platforms dominate distribution; local e-wallets matter for payments; data protection laws differ per country.

## MENA / Gulf
- Statistics: national statistics authorities. Notes: Arabic-language products, local payment methods, country-specific data laws (e.g., Saudi PDPL, UAE PDPL).

## Latin America
- Statistics: national offices (e.g., IBGE Brazil, INEGI Mexico). Law: LGPD (Brazil). Notes: local payment methods (e.g., Pix in Brazil), installments culture.

## Global-only founders
If the user has no local market, pick the two most relevant regions for the idea (by where the target users are and where competitors are weakest) and compare those.
